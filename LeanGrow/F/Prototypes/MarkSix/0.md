We can treat \all goals like normal ones. They will never unify, as we don't expect \all in our forward context. They will not be targets for backsteps for this same reason, but they can a priori be targets of back-rewrite steps. If they contain lnodes in types, we do nothing, hoping that via unification propagation, new inherited goals will be derived from it, that have no lnodes in biders anymore.

If a goal has \all with no lnodes in binders we replace it by its head and gather binder types (telescoping so that the relevant bvars become gnodes refeing to the newly introduced ones). For assembly, we should take note of this, and of the gnodes introduced this way. These gnodes won't have values. At assembly, we build the solutions of the goal, and we add lambdas at the top of each, and replace the concerned gnodes with bvars.

Ltx can now be represented as a tree where nodes correspond to a collection of gnodes w. types and information on which goal (ids) these gnodes have meaning in. The tree structure is due to the fact that we can have, in a certain context where \all were introduces to the forward context, a backstep that spwans a new forall goal. Now, all gnodes of the previous context also have meaning in the new one, but the converse isn't true.

A forward step now corresponds to choosing a node in the tree and considering its ancestors. With that list, we run state-query and embedding-queries as is described below in the "brain discharge". We must then look at the gnodes used as arguments, and add the embedded result to the node that is the youngest wrt. to the youth of the gnodes-args wrt. the tree. Only the leaves of the tree  should be considered, as a thm that embeds in a subcontext will also embed in a context originating from the leaf.
